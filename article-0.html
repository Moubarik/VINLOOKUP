What Is a VIN Number?

A vehicle identification number, commonly called a VIN number, is the unique identifier assigned to a modern road vehicle. For vehicles covered by current U.S. VIN requirements, the VIN is a 17-character identifier that encodes details about the vehicle. NHTSA provides a public VIN decoder that can identify information such as the manufacturer and plant of manufacture.

The VIN decoder is different from a full VIN check. A VIN decoder reads information encoded in the 17-digit VIN itself, while a vehicle history report attempts to connect that VIN number with events recorded later in the vehicle's life, such as title changes, salvage title records, mileage readings, theft records, registrations, recalls, sales, or accident history.

Where is the VIN number location? The most common VIN plate is visible through the lower driver's side of the windshield. The vehicle identification number can also appear on the driver's door jamb, registration document, vehicle title, and sometimes the insurance card.

Free VIN Lookup vs Paid Vehicle History Reports

A free VIN lookup and a paid vehicle history report solve slightly different problems. Free tools are useful for rapidly screening multiple cars, while paid reports may combine a wider set of commercial and government data sources into one report.

CARFAX: $49.99 for one report, $69.99 for two reports, or $119.99 for four reports at the current published consumer pricing.

AutoCheck: $29.99 for one report or $59.99 for five reports through its standard current offer.

NHTSA: free VIN decoder and free recall lookup. NICB VINCheck: free stolen vehicle check and insurance-based salvage or flood screening.
