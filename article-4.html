Why the VIN Matters

The 17-digit vehicle identification number is the only consistent key that ties a physical car to title records, insurance claims, theft reports, recall campaigns, and odometer filings. Locate it on the windshield, door jamb, title, registration, and insurance card. Any discrepancy between those locations ends the deal until resolved.

Free Sources Worth Using

NHTSA VIN decoder and recall lookup (fully free). NICB VINCheck for stolen and insurance-reported salvage/flood vehicles (free). Independent free VIN lookup sites that surface available title or accident signals. NMVTIS-approved paid providers when you need official title-brand depth.

Limitations You Must Accept

No free or paid database sees every private repair, every unreported accident, or every odometer change that never reached a DMV or insurer. A clean free result is a good start, not a guarantee. Combine it with document checks and a physical inspection.

Buyer Types That Gain the Most

Private buyers meeting sellers for the first time, shoppers comparing several dealer listings, auction buyers under time pressure, mechanics preparing inspections, flippers screening inventory volume, and anyone verifying insurance eligibility after a branded title.

Execution Steps

Verify the VIN on the car and paperwork. Run the free lookup. Review title, accident, theft, mileage, and recall flags. Confirm critical hits with NHTSA and NICB. Order paid or NMVTIS data only for finalists. Inspect the vehicle before money changes hands.

That sequence is the lowest-cost way to avoid the most expensive mistakes.
