Decoding vs History

Decoding extracts factory information baked into the 17 characters. History reporting tries to attach everything that happened after the car left the plant. You need both. Free tools cover the decoder side completely via NHTSA and the history side partially via NICB and independent aggregators.

Factory data tells you what the car was supposed to be. History data tells you what has happened to it. The expensive surprises almost always live in the history layer.

Price Pressure of Commercial Reports

CARFAX single reports sit near $50. AutoCheck is lower but still multiplies quickly across five or six candidates. Free screening eliminates the cars that already show salvage, flood, or unrecovered theft so you only buy reports for the ones still alive.

That is the economic logic. Free tools are not a replacement for commercial depth. They are a filter that makes commercial depth affordable by removing the cars that do not deserve it.

Key Red Flags to Watch

Salvage, rebuilt, junk, or flood title brands. Mileage that drops between successive records. NICB theft or insurance-salvage hits. NHTSA open recalls. Sudden registration moves into known flood or severe-weather states. Auction or total-loss events that appear in free sources.

Each of these flags has a different weight. A title brand is almost always a reason to walk away or to demand a large discount and clear insurance answers. An open recall is often fixable at a dealer. A declining odometer sequence needs physical verification and document comparison before any money moves.

Complete Free-First Process

Match the physical VIN to every document. Enter the full number into a free VIN lookup. Capture every signal. Cross-check recalls on NHTSA and theft/salvage on NICB. Escalate only the remaining vehicles to NMVTIS or a commercial report. Finish with a pre-purchase inspection that looks for structural repairs, flood indicators, and mechanical issues.

Free first, paid second, inspection last. That order protects both budget and outcome. It also keeps the research process honest: databases first, then the physical car.
