Core Concepts

The VIN is a 17-character unique identifier. A decoder reads the encoded factory data. A history report links later events to that same identifier. Free tools cover decoding fully and history partially.

Factory data tells you the car’s original specification. History data tells you what has happened since. The expensive problems—salvage brands, total-loss events, theft records, odometer issues—live in the history layer. That is why free history screening is the first practical step.

Commercial Pricing Context

CARFAX single reports hover near $50. AutoCheck offers lower single and package pricing. Five individual paid reports can exceed $150. Free screening changes the math by removing the worst cars before any money is spent.

The goal is not to avoid paid reports. The goal is to stop buying paid reports on cars that free data already flags as salvage, flood, stolen, or title-branded. Once those cars are removed, the remaining budget goes toward deeper research and a real inspection on the cars that still matter.

Signals Available at Zero Cost

Title brands, accident or total-loss flags when reported, odometer sequences that move backward, NICB theft and salvage hits, NHTSA open recalls, ownership or registration patterns, and basic vehicle specifications.

These signals are incomplete by nature. Unreported events stay invisible. The value is speed and cost: free tools catch the problems that were reported, and those problems are often the ones that cost buyers the most money later.

Recommended Sequence

Verify the VIN on the vehicle and documents. Run free lookup. Review every flag. Confirm recalls and theft/salvage with official free sources. Escalate only survivors to paid or NMVTIS reports. Inspect before purchase.

That workflow is the most efficient way to combine free data, paid depth, and physical verification. It keeps research honest and spending focused on cars that have already passed the free filters.
