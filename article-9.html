What the Numbers Mean

Seventeen characters uniquely identify the vehicle. A decoder extracts manufacturer and configuration data. A history report attaches later events. Free sources handle decoding completely and history partially.

Factory data answers “what was this car when it was built?” History data answers “what has happened to it since?” The expensive surprises almost always live in the second answer.

Cost Math

Five CARFAX singles approach $250. AutoCheck packages reduce that, but free screening still removes the cars that already carry salvage, flood, or unrecovered-theft flags so you never pay for those reports at all.

That is the economic case for free tools. They do not replace commercial depth. They make commercial depth affordable by eliminating the cars that free data already shows are high-risk.

What Free Coverage Includes

Available title brands, reported accident or total-loss events, declining mileage sequences, NICB theft and insurance-salvage hits, NHTSA open recalls, ownership or registration signals, and factory specifications.

Coverage is incomplete by design. Unreported events stay invisible. The practical value is speed: free tools catch the reported problems quickly, and those problems are often the ones that cost the most money later.

Execution Order

1. Locate and match the VIN. 2. Run free VIN lookup. 3. Review title, accident, theft, mileage, recall. 4. Confirm with NHTSA and NICB. 5. Escalate survivors to paid or NMVTIS. 6. Inspect. 7. Buy only after clearance.

That sequence is the lowest-cost path to the highest-confidence purchase decision. Free tools do the first filter. Paid tools deepen the research. The mechanic confirms the physical reality of the car that databases cannot fully see.
